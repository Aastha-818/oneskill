# Blog Post

## Find the angle
A topic is not a post. Write the one sentence a reader would repeat to a colleague afterwards. If it is something everyone already agrees with, find the sharper version: the exception, the cost nobody mentions, the specific number.

## Structure
- **Title** - promise something specific. Prefer concrete nouns and numbers over cleverness.
- **Opening** - start in the middle: a scene, a surprising fact, or the claim itself. Cut any first paragraph that begins with "In today's world".
- **Body** - three to five sections, each making one point that supports the angle, each with an example, number or story. Subheads should be readable as a summary on their own.
- **Ending** - say what to do or think differently. Do not summarise what was just read.

## Sentences
- Specific beats general: name the tool, the price, the person, the year.
- Prefer verbs to nouns ("we decided", not "a decision was made").
- Vary sentence length. Read it aloud; rewrite anything you stumble on.
- Cut hedges, throat-clearing and every "very".

## Before publishing
Check each claim has support, each section earns its place, and the first 100 words would make a stranger keep reading. Match the publication's length - if unknown, 800 to 1,200 words.
