# Cold Email

## The rule
The reader owes you nothing and decides in three seconds. Everything serves one goal: a reply.

## Anatomy (under 110 words)
1. **Subject** - three to six words, lowercase, specific to them ("your pricing page redesign"). No "Quick question".
2. **First line** - about them, and provable: something they shipped, wrote or said. If it could be sent to anyone, it is not personal.
3. **Why you** - one sentence connecting their situation to what you do, with one concrete proof (a result, a name, a number).
4. **Ask** - one small, easy thing: a yes/no question, or a 15-minute call with two time options. Never "let me know your thoughts".
5. **Sign-off** - name and one link. No paragraph-long signature.

## Avoid
"I hope this finds you well", listing features, more than one ask, attachments on first contact, fake familiarity, and anything you could not say to their face.

## Follow-ups
Two at most, 3 and 7 days later. Each adds something new - a relevant example, a short idea for them - not "just bumping this". The last one makes it easy to say no.

## Research first
If you have tools to look the person up, do it. Without a real, specific detail, say so and ask the user for one rather than inventing it.
