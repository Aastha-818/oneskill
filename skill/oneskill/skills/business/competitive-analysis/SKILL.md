# Competitive Analysis

## Frame it by the decision
Competitor analysis is for deciding something: where to position, what to build, how to sell against someone. State the decision first; it tells you which competitors and which dimensions matter.

## Who counts
- **Direct** - same product, same customer.
- **Indirect** - different product, same job done.
- **The default** - spreadsheets, an agency, doing nothing. Often the real competitor.
Choose four to six. More becomes a list, not an analysis.

## What to collect, with sources
For each: who they target, their headline promise (their own words), pricing and packaging, key capabilities, how they sell and acquire customers, what customers praise and complain about in reviews, recent moves (funding, launches, hires).
Use current sources and cite them. Mark anything you could not verify, and date it - this goes stale quickly.

## Analyse
- A comparison table on the five or six dimensions buyers actually choose on.
- A positioning map on the two axes that matter most to the target customer.
- For each competitor: where they are strong, and where they are structurally weak - something they cannot easily fix because of their business model or customer base.
- The gap: a customer segment or need nobody serves well.

## Conclude
Give a recommendation tied to the decision, the evidence for it, and what would change your mind. Do not end on a neutral summary.
