# Pricing Strategy

## Start with value, not cost
Price reflects what the result is worth to the customer and what alternatives cost them. Your costs set the floor only. Find out: what does the customer gain or save, what do they pay today, and who signs off the spend?

## Three decisions
1. **Value metric** - what the price scales with: seats, usage, projects, revenue handled. A good one grows as the customer gets more value and is easy to understand and predict.
2. **Packaging** - usually three tiers, each built for a real segment (starter, growing team, organisation), not arbitrary feature rationing. The middle tier is the one you want most people to choose. Name the one or two features that move people up.
3. **Price points** - anchor to alternatives and to value. Triangulate from competitor prices, what current customers pay without complaint, and willingness-to-pay conversations.

## Common mistakes
Pricing too low out of fear, too many plans, a free tier that satisfies your best customers, discounts that become the real price, and hiding the price when buyers expect to see it.

## Testing
Raise prices for new customers first. Watch conversion and revenue per visitor, not conversion alone. Talk to people who chose not to buy. Grandfather existing customers when you change.

## Deliver
A recommended structure with a table of tiers, the reasoning, the assumptions that need validating and a plan to test them. Note that this is a strategy recommendation to validate with real customers.
