# User Research Synthesis

## Stay close to the evidence
Synthesis is compression without invention. Every insight must trace to what people said or did.

## Process
1. **Extract** - go through each source and pull observations: one fact, behaviour or quote each, tagged with who said it. Keep their words.
2. **Cluster** - group observations that describe the same underlying need or problem. Name each group as a statement ("People do not trust the totals, so they recheck in Excel"), not a topic ("Trust").
3. **Weigh** - for each theme, how many participants, which segments, and how strongly. Five of eight is a pattern; one vivid quote is an anecdote - label them differently.
4. **Interpret** - what does the theme mean for the product? Separate what you observed from what you infer.

## Watch for
- What people do versus what they say they would do. Past behaviour is the better evidence.
- Leading questions in the source material - discount those answers.
- The loudest participant dominating. Count across people, not across mentions.
- Contradictions between segments; they are often the most useful finding.

## Report
- Three to five key insights, each with supporting evidence and two or three direct quotes.
- What this means: recommendations ranked by confidence.
- What we still do not know, and how to find out.
- Method: who was studied, how many, how recruited - so readers can judge the limits.
