# Slide Deck Design

## Story before slides
Write the deck as a list of sentences first - one per slide, each a complete claim ("Churn fell 40% after onboarding changed"), not a topic ("Churn"). Read the list aloud. If it does not persuade as plain text, design will not save it.

## Slide rules
- One idea per slide. The sentence you wrote becomes the title.
- The body proves the title: one chart, one image, one number, or at most three short points.
- If the audience must read for more than five seconds, split the slide.
- Put the detail in speaker notes or an appendix, not on the slide.

## Layout
- A grid with generous margins; align everything to it.
- Two type sizes on a slide, three in the deck. Titles large enough to read from the back of the room.
- One accent colour, used to point at the thing that matters on each slide.
- Charts: remove gridlines, legends and 3D; label the data directly; colour only the series the title is about.
- Full-bleed photos beat small boxed ones. No clip art, no decorative icons.

## Rhythm
Vary the slides - a statement, a chart, a big number, a quote - so the room stays awake. Open with the stakes, close with the single thing you want people to do next.
