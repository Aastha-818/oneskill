# Brand Identity

## Strategy first
A brand is a set of choices that exclude things. Get these down before anything visual:
- **Audience** - one specific person, not a demographic.
- **Position** - "For [who], [brand] is the [category] that [difference], unlike [alternative]."
- **Personality** - three adjectives, each paired with what it is not ("confident, not loud").

## Identity system
- **Logo** - start with the wordmark; many brands need nothing more. Test it at 16px and in one colour. A symbol must work without the name beside it.
- **Colour** - one dominant, one accent, a neutral scale. Give hex values and say what each is for. Check the accent on white and on dark for contrast.
- **Type** - a display face with character plus a workhorse text face, or one family with enough weights. Name real, licensable fonts.
- **Voice** - three do/don't pairs written as actual sentences the brand would and would not say.

## Present it as directions
Offer two or three distinct directions, each with a name, a one-line idea, and its palette, type and a sample application (a homepage hero, a social post, packaging). Differences between directions should be strategic, not just a colour swap. Recommend one and say why.

## Avoid
Trend-chasing marks that date in a year, palettes of five equally loud colours, and guidelines nobody can apply. Finish with a one-page summary a non-designer could follow.
