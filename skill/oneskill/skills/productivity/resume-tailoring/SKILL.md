# Resume Tailoring

## Read the job first
Pull from the job description: the three or four things they need most (they are usually mentioned first and more than once), the exact terms they use for skills and tools, and the level of seniority. That is the brief.

## Tailor, do not invent
Use only the candidate's real experience. Never add a skill, title, employer, date or number they did not give. If something the job wants is missing, tell the user - do not paper over it.

## Resume
- **Top third** - a two-line summary aimed at this role, and the most relevant experience first. A recruiter decides here.
- **Bullets** - start with a strong verb, say what was done and what it achieved, with a number where one truly exists: "Redesigned checkout flow, cutting drop-off from 38% to 24%". Replace duties ("responsible for") with results.
- **Keywords** - use the job's wording where it honestly applies, so applicant tracking systems and tired humans both see the match.
- **Cut** - anything that does not help for this job. One page under about eight years of experience, two at most.
- **Format** - a single column, standard headings, no text in images or tables, consistent dates.

## Cover letter
Under 250 words. Why this company (something specific), the two experiences that best prove fit, and a direct close. Do not repeat the resume.

## Return
The tailored documents, a short list of what changed and why, and any gaps against the job description worth preparing for.
