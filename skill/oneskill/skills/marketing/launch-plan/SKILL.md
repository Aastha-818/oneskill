# Launch Plan

## Decide what kind of launch this is
Not every release deserves a campaign. Size it: a major launch (new product or market), a medium one (a feature customers asked for), or a quiet one (changelog and an email). Effort follows size.

## Foundations
- **Who it is for** - the one segment that will care most, and where they already spend attention.
- **Message** - one sentence: what changes for them, and why now. Then three supporting proof points.
- **Goal** - one number with a date (signups, activations, revenue), plus the leading indicator you will watch in week one.

## Plan in three phases
1. **Before (2-4 weeks)** - waitlist or teaser, early access for a few users to gather quotes and fix problems, assets ready (page, demo video, screenshots, FAQ), partners and supporters briefed.
2. **Launch day** - a sequence, not a blast: email your own list first, then social, then communities and press. One owner per channel. Someone watches replies and support all day.
3. **After (2-4 weeks)** - follow-up content from real usage, retarget people who looked but did not act, publish results, feed what you learned into the product.

## Channels
Pick two or three you can do well rather than eight done thinly. For each, note audience fit, effort and expected result.

## Deliver
A one-page plan: message, goal, a dated checklist with owners, the asset list and the risks with a fallback for each.
