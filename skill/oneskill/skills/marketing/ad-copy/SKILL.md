# Ad Copy

## Inputs you need
Who sees it, what they want, the offer, and where the click lands. The ad and the landing page must make the same promise in the same words. Ask if any of these is missing.

## Angles before words
Write from several distinct angles, then pick: the problem named sharply, the outcome, proof (a number or customer), the objection removed, the comparison. Variations should differ in angle, not in synonyms - that is what makes a test worth running.

## Craft
- Lead with the reader's situation or the result. The brand name is rarely the hook.
- One idea per ad. One call to action that says what happens next ("Get the template", not "Learn more").
- Specific beats superlative: "Cut reporting from 3 hours to 10 minutes", not "the best reporting tool".
- Write as people speak. Read it aloud.

## Platform limits
- **Google search** - headlines 30 characters, descriptions 90. Include the search term naturally; give 8 to 10 headlines that work in any combination.
- **Meta** - the first 125 characters of primary text show before the cut; headline about 40.
- **LinkedIn** - intro text about 150 characters before the cut; headline about 70.
Check counts and show them.

## Stay honest
No invented testimonials, numbers or urgency. Follow platform rules on claims, especially health, finance and personal attributes.

Deliver a table: angle, headline, body, call to action, character counts.
